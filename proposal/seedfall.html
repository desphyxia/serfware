Game proposal · Concept v0.9

Seedfall

A settlement game about roads, carriers and small living planets. Build the chain, watch every parcel travel it, and slowly turn a cold rock into a home.

PLATE 01 · Hearth, evening of the first autumnLive render · planet rotates in real time
    The pitch

    Serf City's heart, on a planet that breathes

    In 1993 Serf City (The Settlers) made logistics feel alive. You didn't command people. You laid roads, planted flags, and watched thousands of tiny carriers hand goods to each other until a castle stood where a forest had been.

    Seedfall keeps that exact pleasure: one carrier per road segment, goods passed flag to flag, tools that decide professions, and a territory that grows as your economy does. What changes is the world underneath. Maps become small spherical planets with their own gravity, day length, seasons, tides and weather. Water flows, fire spreads, soil remembers what you grew in it. A star system of very different worlds waits to be settled, and the late game is the slow, visible work of terraforming a planet from rust and frost into forest and rain.

    The tone is cosy without being childish. Nothing shouts at you. Settlers have names, households and memories, and a failing harvest is a problem to solve, not a game-over screen. Depth comes from systems that interact, not from pressure.

    It is built to be played together: friends sharing one keep, neighbours trading across a border, or rival keeps contesting a planet. Combat follows the original closely. Soldiers walk the same roads as bread and timber, so the strongest army is the one with the best supply.

    Pillar

    The chain is the game

    Every log, loaf and ingot is a physical object carried by a person you can follow. Bottlenecks are visible as crowded flags, not red numbers.

    Pillar

    Worlds with weather

    Each planet runs a light simulation of water, wind, heat, soil and life. The map changes under you, and you learn to read it.

    Pillar

    Slow warmth

    Soft failure, readable pacing, pause at any time. Beauty and comfort are mechanics, and the settlement sounds and looks happier as it thrives.

    Pillar

    Built to share

    Every planet is made for one to eight players. Co-op shares roads and megaprojects; PvP is fought over lanterns, supply roads and Star Wells.

    Core loop

    Six verbs, repeated at three scales

    Settle. Place buildings on buildable tiles; builders and levellers prepare the ground.

    Connect. Draw roads between flags. Carriers take their posts.

    Produce. Raw goods become refined goods. Distribution sliders decide who gets scarce coal.

    Grow. Warden lanterns extend the border; new ground brings new resources.

    Understand. Geologists, weather-watchers and naturalists fill the Almanac, which unlocks buildings and forecasts.

    Transform. Dams, irrigation, reforestation and, later, planetary terraforming reshape the land.

    In the first hour the loop runs over minutes around one keep. By mid-game it runs over seasons across a continent. In the late game it runs over centuries across a star system, with the same verbs.

    Signature system

    Roads, flags and carriers

    Plate 02 · Planner view, liveEach road segment has exactly one carrier. Goods wait on flags (max 8) until the next carrier takes them. Amber rings mark flags that are filling up.
    Rule

    One carrier, one segment

    Long roads are slow; flags split them into segments so more carriers work in parallel. This single rule creates the whole puzzle, and it stays untouched.

    Rule

    Eight goods per flag

    A full flag blocks the segment behind it. Jams propagate backward, and you see them as piles of crates and hear them as impatient carriers.

    New

    Road tiers

    Dirt path, gravel, cobble, boardwalk. Better surfaces raise speed and resist mud, snow and flood. Upgrades cost stone and a road crew's time.

    New

    Desire paths

    Settlers walking off-road to homes and wells wear visible paths in grass. The game suggests these as road plans, so the town teaches you its own layout.

    New

    Other carriers

    Pack animals double a busy segment. Barges run canals, ropeways cross gorges, ice roads open on frozen lakes, gliders ride updrafts in low gravity.

    New

    Priority and depots

    Mark goods as urgent or bulk. Widen a crossroads flag into a depot that holds 24 items and sorts them, at the cost of two keepers.

    People

    Settlers are people you can follow

    Every settler is simulated. Click anyone to follow them through their day: breakfast, the walk to the flag, a shift of carrying, an evening at the well. The camera drops to street level and the music thins to what they would hear.

    1. 01

      Arrival or birth

      From the Hearthship's cryo-hold, or born to a household with a home and enough Glow.

    2. 02

      Child

      Follows parents to work. Picks up an affinity for what they watched.

    3. 03

      Carrier

      Everyone starts on the roads. It's how settlers learn the map.

    4. 04

      Apprentice

      Receives a tool. A saw makes a sawyer, a pick a miner, as in the original.

    5. 05

      Master

      Skill raises speed and quality. Masters sign their work and earn titles.

    6. 06

      Elder

      Stops working, teaches apprentices faster, tells stories at festivals.

    7. 07

      Remembered

      A memorial tree grows where they lived. Their journal stays in the Almanac.

    Needs

    Food, warmth, rest, belonging

    Kept simple and visible. Food variety matters more than quantity. Warmth matters on cold worlds, shade on hot ones.

    Households

    Homes are optional, then essential

    Early settlers sleep in the keep. Homes raise birth rate and Glow, and households pass trades down generations.

    Traits

    Shaped by their world

    Settlers raised on Tessel swim; those raised on Rime shrug off cold. Moving people between planets mixes these traits.

    Production

    Chains you can read at a glance

    Chains are short enough to hold in your head and long enough to interlock. Buildings are in italics; goods are in pills. The distribution sliders from the original return: when coal is scarce, you decide whether the smelter, the toolsmith or the glassworks gets it.

    Distribution

    Sliders per scarce material

    Food to mines, coal to forges, planks to shipwright or toolsmith, steel to tools or bells. Save them as seasonal presets.

    Storage

    Things spoil and weather

    Fish spoils in days unless smoked or iced. Timber stored outside warps in rain. Granaries attract mice, and a cat fixes that.

    Quality

    Masterwork goods

    Skilled workers sometimes produce masterwork items. They last longer, raise Glow and are prized in interplanetary trade.

    Planets

    Small worlds, walkable in an evening

    Each map is a whole planet you can orbit with the camera. Curvature is part of play: the horizon is close, your first village sits on a gentle dome, and by mid-game your roads wrap around to meet themselves.

    Physics follows the planet. Gravity changes carrier speed and load, how tall a tower can stand, how far water jets and sparks fly. Rotation sets day length. Axial tilt gives seasons that differ by latitude, so a colony near the pole lives with long summer days and long winter nights. Moons raise tides. A tidally locked world has a permanent noon, a permanent midnight, and a habitable twilight ring.

    Star Wells

    Twelve on every planet, never in the same places

    Geometry forces one rule. A sphere tiled with only hexagons and pentagons always needs exactly twelve pentagons (it follows from Euler's formula). The standard grid puts them at the corners of an icosahedron, 63.4° apart, so every planet would have the same pattern.

    Seedfall builds each grid from the planet's seed. It scatters points, relaxes them into hexagons, and steers the twelve defects to places the generator chooses: a wide scatter on one world, a tight cluster on another, a chain along a mountain ridge on a third. Placement rules keep them apart from start sites and from each other. The twelve pentagons become Star Wells: ancient sites with ruins, rare resources and the victory points for PvP. Because their layout differs on every planet, so does each planet's strategic map.

    Biomes

    Eight biomes, each with its own rule

    A biome is more than a palette. Each one changes a core system, so settling somewhere new means relearning something you thought you knew.

    Beneath

    Geology you can see in section

    Plate 13 · Section through the Hearth foothillsGeologists walk out and plant signposts; the coloured board shows what lies below. Hold Alt in game to slice the ground like this anywhere.
    Kept

    Geologist signposts

    Send a geologist to a flag; they wander and plant signs. It is slow, charming and still the best way to find a seam.

    New

    Strata and depletion

    Veins have real volume and deplete. Deep ores need timber-propped shafts; cheap shafts collapse and close.

    New

    Groundwater and soil

    Wells draw from aquifers that fall in drought. Soil tracks nitrogen, moisture and compaction; crop rotation and hedgerows keep fields fertile.

    A world that keeps moving

    Late game

    Terraforming takes centuries, and you watch every one

    Terraforming is a slow planetary project that runs alongside your settlements. Time scales up once a colony is self-sufficient: a season passes in minutes, then a decade. Your towns keep running at their own pace, and the planet around them changes colour. Drag the slider to see one valley on Rime change over six hundred years.

    Heat & air

    Warm it, thicken it

    Orbital mirrors, greenhouse-gas works, redirected ice comets. Each is a megaproject built by your economy, with goods launched from other worlds.

    Water

    Make it rain

    Melt caps, seed clouds, dig lake basins before the thaw so water pools where you want it. The water sim takes it from there.

    Life

    Seed, then step back

    Lichen, moss, grasses, pioneer trees, pollinators, grazers. You choose the seed mix; succession and climate decide what thrives.

    Kinship

    Native life has a say

    Some worlds already have life. Terraforming can erase it. Reserves, gene banks and hybrid biomes let you choose, and the Almanac remembers what you chose.

    Local

    Small-scale shaping

    Before planets, valleys: domes, heated greenhouses, windbreak forests and irrigation shape microclimates from the first hour.

    Endgame

    Bloom

    A planet that sustains itself earns a Bloom: settlers walk outside unaided, rain falls on its own and the soundtrack gains a new theme.

    Colonisation

    From one keep to a star system

    1. 01

      Survey

      Probes map a world's tiles, climate and hazards. Unscanned areas stay as sketches.

    2. 02

      Pack the Hearthship

      Choose settlers, tools, seeds and stock within a mass budget. What you pack is your start.

    3. 03

      Land

      Pick a site by slope, water and ore. The ship unpacks into a keep and first store.

    4. 04

      Take root

      Reach food, shelter and tools on site. The colony earns Rooted status and its own policies.

    5. 05

      Build a launch rail

      Mass drivers throw bulk cargo to orbit; skyships carry people and fragile goods.

    6. 06

      Trade and send

      Launch windows open as worlds align. Colonies specialise and depend on each other.

    Logistics

    Carriers all the way up

    Interplanetary cargo still leaves by road. Crates travel flag to flag to the launch rail, so an orbital shipment can be held up by a muddy lane.

    Culture

    Colonies drift

    Over generations each colony develops customs, songs, building styles and a slight dialect in settler chatter, shaped by its world.

    Governance

    Policies per colony

    Work hours, festival calendars, what to export, how much to keep. Set them yourself or name a steward to run a mature colony.

    Conflict

    Combat is logistics with consequences

    Fights are won the same way as everything else in Seedfall: on the roads.

    Soldiers are settlers given arms made by your own economy. Reinforcements, food and weapons all travel flag to flag, so a strong economy with a well-lit road network beats a large army at the end of a muddy lane. The core comes straight from Serf City: you send soldiers from your buildings to an enemy building, and they duel at its door one at a time until one side runs out. Seedfall keeps that and makes everything that decides a duel visible before you commit: rank, arms, resolve, fatigue and ground.

    Plate 15 · A border assault, liveTerritory is light. Amber wardens march from a beacon tower, duel the lamp house's garrison at the door, and take it. The border redraws, and a blue workshop inside the new border goes dark and becomes stranded.

    Who fights

    Made from spear + shield · steel, timber

    Wardens

    The backbone. They garrison lantern buildings and fight the duels at the door. Five ranks, earned by winning duels or by training at the keep at a cost in gold.

    Made from bow · timber, flax string

    Rangers

    Posted in beacon towers and keeps. They don't duel. They loose volleys at attackers crossing their sight, adding fatigue before the fight reaches the door. Night and fog blind them.

    Made from saddle + mount · stable, grain

    Outriders

    Fast and lightly armed. They raid unlit road flags: an outrider standing on your flag stops goods from passing. They can't take buildings. They are the reason you light long roads.

    Made from tools · timber, stone

    Engineers

    Build palisades, bridges and field camps. A field camp is a temporary flag with food that removes fatigue for attackers, and it makes long offensives possible.

    An assault, step by step

    1. 01

      Scout

      You only see what your light and scouts see. Enemy garrisons show as estimates until you look closer.

    2. 02

      Muster

      Pick a target in range and how many wardens each of your buildings sends. Every building keeps at least one.

    3. 03

      March

      Soldiers walk. Distance from your nearest store, slopes, snow and ranger volleys add fatigue.

    4. 04

      Duel at the door

      Defenders step out one at a time. Each duel is a readable exchange of a few blows. Winners stay, tired.

    5. 05

      Capture

      When the last defender falls or yields, the building changes hands, its light changes colour and the border redraws.

    6. 06

      Aftermath

      Enemy buildings now inside your border are stranded, not burned. The wounded walk home. Anyone cut off is taken prisoner.

    Kept from 1993

    Gold raises resolve

    Gold and treasures in your storehouses raise every soldier's resolve, as in the original. So do high Glow, a fed garrison and fighting on home ground. Recent losses lower it.

    Supply

    Lit roads are safe roads

    Garrisons eat. A lamp house whose food road is cut loses resolve each day. Outriders can hold unlit flags, so lantern posts along your trade roads are defence too.

    Stakes setting

    Wounded or mortal

    Wounded (default): losers retreat and recover at a keep over a season. Mortal: they die, and a memorial tree marks the place. Lobbies choose the setting.

    Losses

    Stranded, not burned

    Serf City burned buildings in lost land. Here they go dark: workers carry what they can home. Retake the land within a season to restore them, or the new owner can reopen them at half cost.

    World

    Night, weather, terrain

    Night hides a march from towers. Fog blinds rangers. Mud and snow add fatigue. Rivers funnel attacks onto bridges, and tides on Tessel open and close routes twice a day.

    Across planets

    Beachheads and windows

    An invasion must land outside enemy light and build a beachhead keep. Skyships arrive only in launch windows, so the defender knows when to expect them. There is no bombardment from orbit.

    Together

    Every planet size is tuned for a player count, and every system in this document works with several keeps on one world. Co-op and PvP use the same simulation. The difference is who owns which lanterns.

    Victory

    Ways to win

    Conquest (take every keep), Star Wells (hold a majority of a planet's twelve for one full year), Bloom, Prosperity (a production target) or Influence (win over the most hamlets). Hosts can combine them.

    Pacing

    Peace clock

    No attacks before a set time, from 0 to 3 in-game years. Builders get their cosy opening, and the tension rises as the clock runs down.

    Diplomacy

    Pacts the game enforces

    Truces, trade pacts, shared roads and prisoner exchanges are signed in game. Breaking one costs Glow and leaves a mark on your reputation that other players can see.

    Fairness

    Balanced starts on uneven worlds

    Planets aren't symmetric, so the generator scores each start site for water, ore, soil and distance to Star Wells, then re-rolls until the sites are within 5 % of each other. Ranked play uses curated seeds.

    Comebacks

    Losing slowly, recovering fast

    Defenders have home-ground bonuses. Attackers tire far from their stores. Stranded buildings can be restored. When a keep is threatened, settlers volunteer as militia.

    Netcode

    Lockstep, drop-in, no lost games

    Deterministic lockstep with a checksum every tick. Players can join co-op mid-game, and an AI steward takes over a disconnected player's keep until they return. Replays and spectating come with it.

    Neighbours

    Nomads, hamlets, natives

    Every mode can include neutral life. Nomad traders bring seeds and knowledge, hamlets join the most welcoming keep, and native creatures share the land.

    Async

    Letters and skyships

    Between sessions, send cargo to a friend's system. It arrives hours later in real time, with a letter and a postcard.

    Solo

    AI rivals

    AI keeps use the same rules and personalities: the Builder, the Trader, the Warden. They fill empty seats in any mode.

    Knowledge

    The Almanac replaces the tech tree

    There is no research screen with points. Knowledge comes from settlers noticing things. The first time a geologist finds copper, an Almanac page appears with a sketch, and the coppersmith becomes buildable. After a full year of weather records, you get forecasts. Watch deer for a season and a naturalist learns to domesticate them.

    The Almanac is also the game's journal. It fills with hand-drawn plates of species, ores, weather and buildings, with settler notes in the margins. Precursor ruins on some worlds hold pages you cannot read until you have learned enough to understand them.

    Unlock by doing

    Observation

    Mine, fish, farm, survive a winter. Each first adds a page, and pages combine into blueprints.

    Unlock by asking

    Teaching

    Elders and nomads teach techniques. Masters invent variations on their own buildings.

    Unlock by digging

    Ruins

    Excavate precursor sites for seed vaults, star charts and the old terraforming methods.

    Wellbeing

    Glow: comfort as a mechanic

    Glow is the settlement's mood, shown as the warmth of window light across town. It has five parts, and each one is earned through the economy rather than bought.

    PartRaised byEffect when high

    Festivals

    A calendar per world

    First Harvest, Longest Night, Tide Turn. Festivals pause work for a day, cost food and raise Belonging for a season.

    Music

    The town writes its soundtrack

    Each workshop adds a layer to the adaptive score: the smithy on the downbeat, the mill as a drone, the choir when Glow is high.

    Decoration

    Freeform prettiness

    Paint shutters, plant flower boxes, place lanterns. It is optional, it adds Beauty, and it makes the photo mode worth using.

    Challenge

    Adversity that asks for a plan

    Hardships arrive with warning, are shaped by how you built, and leave something behind. Three settings: Gentle (no losses of people), Honest (the default) and Hard (colonies can fail).

    Structure

    Modes and progression

    Multiplayer modes are covered under Multiplayer. These are the frames that hold them.

    Campaign · solo or co-op

    The Long Voyage

    Twelve chapters across the Lanterne system, from the first landing on Hearth to the Bloom of Rime. Each chapter teaches one system and one world. Two to four players can play it together, and three chapters are contested by AI rivals.

    Sandbox

    Seeded systems

    Generate a system from a seed with sliders for planet count, harshness, native life and rivals. Share seeds as short words.

    Scenarios

    Handmade puzzles

    Rebuild after a flood, supply a lighthouse through winter, or hold a bridge against a stronger neighbour.

    Creative

    World painter

    Sculpt planets, place biomes and Star Wells, write scenarios and share them through the workshop. Full mod support for buildings, goods and units.

    Art & audio direction

    A painted miniature under a real sky

    The look combines a handmade diorama with modern light. Buildings are chunky and hand-textured, settlers are readable at a distance, and physically based atmospheric scattering, soft global illumination and volumetric clouds make every hour of the day distinct. A gentle tilt-shift focus keeps the planet feeling small and precious.

    Voices

    Settler chatter

    A small invented language, gentle and wordless in tone, with colony dialects. Work songs rise when Glow is high.

    Camera

    Photo and follow modes

    Street-level follow cam, time-lapse of a whole season, a planetary orbit view, and postcards you can send with async letters.

    Technology

    How the planets are built

    Grid

    Seeded hexes on a sphere

    Each planet's grid is generated from its seed: scattered points, relaxed into hexagons, with the twelve required pentagons steered to seeded positions. Tile areas vary by a few percent, so the simulation weights water, soil and territory by each tile's real area. Rare pentagon–heptagon pairs can mark fault lines.

    Simulation

    Deterministic and layered

    A fixed-point simulation at 10 ticks per second runs the economy and settlers for lockstep co-op. Water, heat and erosion run on the GPU at coarser rates and sync as snapshots.

    Scale

    Twelve thousand settlers per planet

    Entity-component architecture; settlers far from the camera are simulated on the road graph only and animated as impostors. Idle planets run in summary mode.

    Rendering

    Modern, scalable

    Clustered lighting for thousands of lanterns, virtual texturing for per-tile wear, probe-based GI, temporal upscaling. A handheld preset keeps 30 fps with the same simulation.

    Saves

    A world journal

    Saves are compact event logs plus snapshots, so a colony's whole history can replay as a time-lapse.

    Access

    Accessibility from day one

    Pause and speed at any time, colour-blind palettes for goods, remappable controls, controller-first radial menus, scalable text and optional subtitles for chatter.

    Production

    Roadmap and risks

    1. Phase 0 · 4 mo

      Roads on a ball

      Flags, carriers and five buildings on a seeded spherical grid, two players in lockstep from day one.

    2. Phase 1 · 8 mo

      Hearth slice

      Full Meadowlands economy, settlers, seasons, Glow. 2-player co-op and duel combat. Art target locked.

    3. Phase 2 · 8 mo

      Living world

      Water, fire, wind, soil, ecology. Almanac. Two more biomes.

    4. Phase 3 · 10 mo

      The system

      Colonisation, launch rails, all eight biomes, team PvP and relay co-op. Early access.

    5. Phase 4 · 8 mo

      Bloom

      Terraforming, campaign, Bloom race, async letters, modding. Release 1.0.
